This page lists every change to the verdict rules, so you can see why a move's verdict might differ from the last time you looked, along with the bigger new features. Newest first. When a rule changes, the reviews you've already saved are graded again with the new rules.

## 18 November 2026

- Point at Play or Puzzles in the sidebar for shortcuts straight to each way to play and each puzzle mode.
- The computer you play is now called the coach, everywhere: Play the coach.
- Play has a new front, like Home's: Play online with your time a press away, then the coach and a friend.
- Puzzles on a computer opens on its own start page: what the coach suggests today, rated puzzles as the big card, then Daily, Sprint and your mistakes. Each mode now has a page of its own instead of tabs, with the same tall panel as Play and Analysis, and the coach talks to you in a speech bubble.
- Under Highlight last move in Board settings: pick its colour, or any colour of your own, and how strong it is, so it stands out on your board.
- Fixed: Puzzle Sprint kept giving the same puzzles, run after run.

## 3 November 2026

**Rules:** a piece you couldn't have saved isn't a sacrifice. If a fork with check wins one of your pieces whatever you do, the move that answers the check doesn't give it up. Neither does letting go of a pawn your opponent has just attacked. Both used to be called Legendary and are now graded like any other move. Thanks to whoever reported it. Saved reviews are graded again with the new rules.

## 2 November 2026

**Rules:** a capture through the square the taking piece left is a deflection only if you keep what you won. If the piece that captures is taken straight back, it was a trade, not a sacrifice.

**Rules:** between two checkmates, the distance counts. Walking into a faster mate isn't Best, and neither is a slower mate when a faster one was there. A piece left hanging while you force mate isn't a sacrifice if the best defence never takes it.

**Rules:** Clutch needs every other move to give up at least 9% of your winning chance, down from 10%. Only the move that starts a forced mate is Clutch; the moves that carry it out are Best.

**Rules:** a move that falls 3% or more short of the engine's best is searched deeper before it gets a verdict (it was 5%). A stronger move can look closer than it is at review depth. Checked against a much stronger engine on recent games, more than twice as many of the verdicts this changed now agree with it.

Saved reviews are graded again with the new rules. The deeper search can't be redone from what a review saves, so a game reviewed before today is reviewed again the first time you open it.

- Reviews on a desktop run up to eight engines at once, and take about a third less time.
- Review many games at once on desktop. With two or more games unreviewed, Home's button becomes Review latest games: pick a date range, see how many games that is and about how long they'll take, and each game is saved as it finishes.
- Explain no longer gets stuck on a position, and leaving it no longer blanks the page in newer versions of Chrome.
- If a page does crash, it now says what went wrong instead of showing a blank screen.

## 27 October 2026

**Rules:** a move is now graded against what the game went on to show, not only the engine's first look. Before a move is called a mistake, every search that ranks it has to agree, and opening positions take their evals from the Lichess cloud.

**Rules:** Clutch is the only move that keeps what you have. It now also counts an only move whose alternatives would all be Blunders, a capture that gets a piece out of a threat, and a checkmate when every other move gives the game away.

**Rules:** a sacrifice is checked against the moves actually played. A deflection counts, even when the material comes straight back. A piece left to a counter-attack doesn't, and neither does a trade through the taking piece's square. A pawn grabbed when you're a queen down no longer counts as taking the queen back, and the move that allows a checkmate is never Best.

## 21 October 2026

**Rules:** a sacrifice has to be taken at once. If your opponent can only win the material after a quiet move first, your move didn't offer it, so it isn't a sacrifice and can't be Legendary. The game behind this is in [What counts as a sacrifice](/blog/what-counts-as-a-sacrifice).

Saved reviews now keep the engine's work, so a rule change regrades them in the background without running the engine again. Reviews saved before this change are reviewed once more the next time you open them.

## 20 October 2026

**Rules:** Rookahead's verdicts became our own [written rules](https://github.com/rookahead/rookahead/blob/main/docs/rulebook.md). The main changes from before:

- Only the engine's own move is Best. A different move that comes out almost as good is Strong.
- "Hard to find" is judged the way a person plays, not by how deep the engine had to look. Mate in one, promotions, safe captures and moving an attacked piece to safety are easy to find; quiet moves and sacrifices are hard.
- A sacrifice that wins the material back a few moves later through a tactic still counts as a sacrifice.
- A sacrifice your opponent should decline, because taking it loses, can be Legendary even when you were already winning.
- In defence, finding the one move that doesn't get mated is Clutch however obvious it looks. Taking back material your opponent just took is never Clutch.

Also new:

- Rookahead works as a phone app: add it to your home screen, and it opens full screen with a tab bar.
- Colour themes (Sky, Gold, Indigo, Coral, Forest and Ivory) and a Dim mode between dark and light.
- The computer plays more like a person: at the lower levels it makes the mistakes people at that rating make.
